Your organisation’s own allow and deny lists — the decisions you have made about packages, as opposed to what a scanner or a published advisory says about them. Authored is what is written and committed here. Enforced is what firewall replicas report they have loaded. They move on different clocks (a replica re-reads its file every few seconds; this page shows the last heartbeat, up to {{ .StaleAfter }} old), so a change you just made can be authored but not yet enforced. That is expected, and it is the point of showing both.

The published-malware feed is separate, and it wins. FW_MALWARE_LIST is consulted before these lists, so allow-listing a package that a published advisory names does not serve it. That ordering is deliberate: the alternative is an allow list that can serve known malware. The feed is not editable here — it is machine-generated from OSV, and “publicly reported as malicious” and “our organisation blocked this” are different facts that a developer needs to be able to tell apart.
